Boilerplate, three lengths

One line

ROTA is a private, beautiful rotation concierge for watch collectors: every morning it answers which watch today? with taste and context.

Fifty words

ROTA is the daily ritual app for watch collectors. Every morning it answers one question with taste and context — which watch today? — by weighing the occasion, live weather, your wear history, your straps and your own learned preferences. It has no servers, no accounts and no analytics, and it never receives your data.

A hundred and fifty words

ROTA is the daily ritual app for watch collectors. Every morning it answers one question with taste and context — which watch today? — by running a decision graph over the occasion, live weather, wear equity, strap configuration and a six-axis taste engine the owner tunes themselves. It learns from every override. Around that ritual it wraps a gallery-grade collection manager where every watch stands on a soft plinth, a virtual watch box, straps as first-class objects, wear analytics, a drag-ranked dream list with a Next Purchase Fit score, service and accuracy tracking, a headline-only news journal, and a museum-caption PDF lookbook rendered on the device.

It also has no servers, no accounts and no analytics, and not one third-party SDK in the binary. The collection lives on the device and syncs only through the owner's own private iCloud. The App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected.

The three pillars

① The Rotation Graph

Competing apps manage "you haven't worn this in a while". ROTA runs a genuine decision graph every morning: occasion type × live weather × wear equity × strap configuration × learned taste × what the owner actually chose last time for this kind of day. It proposes one hero pick with ranked alternates and a "Why this pick?" sheet that shows the working, and it learns from every override. Below four watches the same engine scores watch and strap together, because a beginner's collection is their straps.

② The Taste Engine

Six weighted scoring axes, brand affinities and style tags produce a personal score for every watch, and a collection-level view: occasion coverage, variety, redundancy, and a Next Purchase Fit ranking over the dream list. Other apps tell you what your watches are worth. ROTA tells you what your collection is missing — framed as chapters rather than gaps, so a two-watch owner is never shamed by a score.

③ Strap-aware wardrobe, and privacy as a feature

Straps are first-class objects — one strap dresses many watches, and it changes what a watch is for. All of it in an editorial, gallery-grade interface, with a Data Not Collected privacy label that is a deliberate, marketable contrast to a category whose largest players monetise their users.

Feature list

  • Today — one hero suggestion on its plinth, occasion chip, weather glyph with Apple Weather attribution, ranked alternates, "Something came up", "Worn it".
  • The Gallery — photography-first grid; typographic Specimen Cards stand in for unphotographed watches so nothing ever looks broken or empty.
  • The Box — a virtual watch box with a ghost slot that always has room for one more. Walnut, noir and saddle.
  • Straps — one strap to many watches, with lug-width-aware ranking that never hides a mismatch.
  • Wrist Time — wear bars, calendar heat-map, occasion history, collection score and coverage matrix.
  • The Vitrine and the Journey — drag-ranked dream list, Next Purchase Fit, and archetype chapters from The One-Watch Life to The Curated Cabinet. Own nothing yet? The taste primer shortlists a first watch.
  • Care — service intervals, warranty expiry, water-resistance retests, and an accuracy logger that charts seconds per day.
  • Chronicle — a wrist-shot journal with plinth-styled share cards, and an annual "your year on the wrist" recap.
  • The Journal — headlines from publishers the owner enables, matched to their watches on the device. Headline, source, date and link only. Never gated.
  • PDF lookbook — a museum-caption catalogue rendered on the device; prices off by default, serials never in a shared document.
  • Admire — full-bleed, chrome-free contemplation with a slow light sweep. On iPad, a desk stand.
  • Accessibility — VoiceOver end to end, Dynamic Type to AX3 on every screen, full Reduce Motion support.

The privacy claim, stated for quotation

The claim, verbatim, and the only wording we use:

ROTA has no servers, no accounts, and no analytics — we never receive your data.

We ask one thing of anyone writing about this. The claim is that nothing reaches us — not that nothing leaves the phone. Please do not upgrade it into the absolute version. The absolute version is a different claim and it is false, and the correction would land on you rather than on us.

Three things do leave, and the app names all three on its own privacy page and in its first-run screen: approximate location goes to Apple for the weather, the collection goes to the owner's own private iCloud, and a feed fetch tells the publisher's server that someone read the feed. The app enumerates them in code, and an automated test fails the build if a fourth is added without the copy naming it — which is the part worth writing about, because it is the part that is hard to quietly falsify.

The full privacy policy is written to be checked rather than trusted, and we would rather be quoted from it than summarised.

Palette and typography

ROTA ships two hand-tuned themes and defaults to the dark one. Heritage is the mood of a private club's watch room at 10 pm; Gallery is a maison's vitrine at noon. Gold is never used for body text in either — accent, rules and numerals only.

  • Ground#0E1512
  • Surface#161D19
  • Champagne#C9A96A
  • Porcelain#F2EFE8
  • Burgundy#7A3B41

Type is Apple's own, deliberately: New York for display — watch names, headlines and tabular numerals — SF Pro Text for body and interface, and SF Mono for reference numbers only, which is the detail collectors notice first.

The one visual idea the app is remembered by is the plinth: a soft radial pedestal with a faint reflective floor under every watch, identical from the grid card to the watch page to the Today card to the Box to the PDF. When ROTA has no photograph it puts a typographic Specimen Card on the same plinth rather than showing a grey rectangle.

What ROTA is not

Stated plainly so that a review does not have to guess:

  • Not a marketplace. Nothing is for sale in ROTA except ROTA. There is no listing, no dealer, no affiliate link and no referral fee anywhere in it.
  • Not a valuation service. Any price in the app is one the owner typed. ROTA performs no market lookups and offers no opinion about what anything is worth.
  • Not an authentication or insurance product. The PDF lookbook is a catalogue, not an appraisal.
  • Not a news reader. The Journal shows headline, source, date and a link, and sends the reader to the publisher's own page. No article text, no images, no reader mode.
  • Not a social network. There are no accounts, no profiles, no following and no feed of other people. Share cards are images the owner exports and posts wherever they like.
  • Not affiliated with any watch brand. Brand and model names identify the watches an owner owns. Nothing more is implied.
