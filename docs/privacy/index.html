Privacy policy

Your collection. Nobody's business.

Applies to ROTA for iPhone and iPad, all versions.
Effective 17 August 2026. Last updated 17 August 2026.

ROTA has no servers, no accounts, and no analytics — we never receive your data.

That sentence is the whole policy. Everything below exists so that you can check it.

Note what the sentence does not say. It does not say that nothing leaves your phone, because that would not be true, and this is an audience that will point a proxy at the app and find out. It says that nothing reaches us — which is true, is the stronger claim, and is exactly what the App Store's Data Not Collected label means.

There is no ROTA account, because there is no ROTA server to hold one. There is no advertising identifier, no attribution framework, and nothing in ROTA sends anything to us, because there is nowhere for it to send anything to.

App Store privacy label: Data Not Collected.

No third-party SDKs are linked into ROTA — not one, not for analytics, not for the paywall. That is what makes the sentence above literally true rather than merely intended.

What leaves this phone

Three things do leave, and you should know all three by name. Nothing else does, and nothing at all comes to us.

Approximate location → Apple

Where it goes
Apple, through WeatherKit.
What is sent
A coarse location — roughly your neighbourhood, never your address — each time ROTA reads the weather. iOS is asked for reduced accuracy at the system level, so a precise fix is never requested and never received.
Why
Rain rests your leather straps and 28° favours a bracelet. Turn the weather assist off in Assists and this stops entirely. The rotation engine renormalises without the weather axis and keeps working.
Who holds it
Apple, under Apple Weather's own privacy terms. We are never told your location, and ROTA never stores it.

Your collection → your own private iCloud

Where it goes
Your iCloud account, in its private database. The container is iCloud.com.jmamblat.rota and it exists inside your Apple Account, not ours.
What is sent
Everything you enter: watches, photographs, straps, wear history, notes, serials and prices.
Why
It is how your iPhone and iPad show the same collection. It is your storage, under your Apple Account — we have no key and no copy. Sign out of iCloud and ROTA keeps working, on this device only.
Who holds it
You do. CloudKit's private database is readable only by the signed-in Apple Account. There is no public database, no shared database, and no server-side code of ours anywhere in the system. We could not read it if we wanted to.

Feed requests → the publishers you enable

Where it goes
Each publisher whose feed you switch on — Hodinkee, Fratello, Monochrome and the rest. Nothing is requested from a publisher whose feed is off.
What is sent
Their server sees your device's IP address and the fact that a feed was fetched. It is not told which watches you own; matching headlines to your collection happens here, on the device.
Why
It is how the Journal has anything in it. Switch a feed off in Journal and nothing is requested from that publisher again.
Who holds it
The publisher, under their own privacy policy — the same as if you had opened their site in Safari. Tapping a headline opens the publisher's own page in a Safari view, at which point their site sees you the way any website does.

Two more things, named for completeness

Neither of these is ROTA sending your data anywhere. Both are the operating system and the App Store doing what they do for every app on your phone. We name them anyway, because a policy that quietly omits the awkward items is not worth reading.

Purchases

If you buy ROTA Pro, Apple handles the entire transaction. We never see your name, your payment details, your address or your Apple Account. StoreKit tells the app one thing — whether an entitlement is currently active — and that answer is cached on the device so the app knows you are Pro even when offline. App Store Connect later shows us aggregate sales figures by country, the same report every developer on the store receives; it contains no identity and nothing about your collection.

Crash reports, only if you have opted in

iOS has a system setting, Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers. If it is on, Apple may include ROTA in the anonymised crash and energy reports it shows developers in App Store Connect. Those reports are a stack trace, an OS version and a device model. They contain nothing you typed, no photographs, no collection data and no identifier that points at you. We do not operate that pipeline, we cannot switch it on for you, and you can switch it off for every app in that same setting. It is the one channel through which any information connected to ROTA can reach us at all, and it carries none of your data.

What stays in the app

Some things are worth stating explicitly because they are the things collectors worry about.

  • Serial numbers and prices stay in the app. They are excluded from the PDF lookbook unless you switch them on at export time, and they are never part of a share card.
  • Photographs live in the app's own storage and in your private iCloud. They are never uploaded anywhere else, never used for anything, and never analysed off the device.
  • Calendar events, if you enable the calendar assist, are read on the device to suggest a day type and are never stored, never synced and never transmitted. The assist is off unless you turn it on.
  • Headline matching — deciding that an article is about a watch you own — happens entirely on the device against the text of the feed. The publisher is never told what you own.
  • “Report a correction” opens your mail app with the catalog row and the app version, and nothing else: no photographs, no collection, no identifiers. You see the message before you send it, and you can edit or discard it.
  • The reference catalog is bundled inside the app. Looking up a reference number never contacts a server; validation is performed on the device against rules shipped in the binary.

The permissions ROTA asks for

Each one is asked for in context, at the moment it becomes useful, and each is optional. Declining any of them leaves the app fully functional with that one assist absent.

That is not a marketing channel: Apple's SwiftData iCloud sync uses silent system pushes to learn that another one of your devices changed something, and the app is terminated at launch without it. No message from us is ever delivered through it.

What the privacy manifest declares

Apple requires apps to declare a reason for certain APIs, and to declare any domains used for tracking. ROTA's manifest declares:

  • Tracking: none. The tracking-domains list is empty. ROTA has never asked for App Tracking Transparency permission because it has nothing to ask for.
  • Collected data types: none.
  • Required-reason APIs: user defaults (CA92.1), file timestamps (C617.1), disk space (E174.1) and system boot time (35F9.1) — reached by settings storage, the image and thumbnail cache, the free-space check before a PDF export, and animation timing respectively. All four are device-local; none of them leaves the device or is linked to you.

Deleting your data

Because we hold nothing, there is nothing for us to delete and no request for you to file. Everything is under your own control:

  • Delete a watch, a photograph or an entry in the app, and it is gone from the device and, on the next sync, from your iCloud.
  • Delete the app and its on-device store goes with it.
  • Delete the iCloud copy in Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage, where ROTA appears as an app using iCloud and can be removed with its data.
  • Take your data with you using the PDF lookbook export, which renders on the device and is yours to share however you like.
  • Stop the weather lookups in ROTA's own Assists settings, or revoke location in iOS Settings.
  • Stop the feed requests by switching feeds off in Journal.

GDPR, UK GDPR and US state privacy laws

These laws grant you rights against whoever holds your personal data. In ROTA's case the honest answer is that we are not that party for anything you enter in the app: we are not a controller or a processor of your collection, because it never comes to us and we have no means of obtaining it.

  • Access, portability, rectification and erasure are all exercised directly in the app and in iOS, as described above, and do not need us.
  • Apple is the party that holds your iCloud data and your weather requests, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Your rights in respect of that data are exercisable against Apple, through Apple's own privacy portal.
  • Publishers whose feeds you enable hold whatever their web servers log, under their own policies.
  • We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined by the California Consumer Privacy Act, or under any other state privacy law. We have none to sell. There is no "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link on this site because there is nothing behind it.
  • No automated decision-making with legal effect takes place. The rotation engine is a scoring function that runs on your device and suggests a watch.

If you want any of this confirmed in writing for a compliance file, write to us and we will confirm it in writing.

Children

ROTA is a general-audience app, rated 4+, with no user-generated content shown to other people, no messaging, no advertising and no data collection. It is not directed at children and it does not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age, because it does not collect anything at all.

This website

This site is static HTML and CSS. It sets no cookies, runs no JavaScript, loads no web fonts, embeds no third-party frames and carries no analytics of any kind. An app whose pitch is "no analytics" cannot honestly ship a site with a tracking tag on it.

It is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub's servers receive the ordinary web-request information — your IP address, the page you asked for, your browser's user-agent — handled under GitHub's privacy statement. We do not receive those logs and cannot access them. The outbound links on this site point to Apple, GitHub and publishers' own pages, and following one takes you to a site with its own policy.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of ROTA ever changes what leaves the device, this page changes first, the app's in-app privacy manifesto changes with it, and the date at the top changes. The app enumerates its egress points in code, and an automated test fails the build if a fourth one is added without the copy being updated to name it. That is the mechanism that makes this page hard to quietly falsify.
