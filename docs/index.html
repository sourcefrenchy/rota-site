ROTA

The most beautiful way to wear your collection.

ROTA is the daily ritual app for watch collectors. Every morning it answers one question with taste and context — which watch today? — and wraps that ritual in a gallery-grade collection manager, a personal taste engine, wear analytics, a dream list and a shareable lookbook.

iPhone and iPad · iOS 26 · Coming to the App Store

Three promises

It knows your life, it knows your taste,
and it knows nothing about you.

It knows your life

Vacation day, office day, beach day, black-tie dinner — plus live weather, your wear history and your own learned preferences — feed a single daily suggestion. Rain rests your leather straps. Twenty-eight degrees favours a bracelet.

It knows your taste

A six-axis scoring engine with weights you tune yourself — I love old money, full bracelets, variety; I'm not a fan of this brand — shapes every recommendation and every "what should I buy next" answer.

It knows nothing about you

No servers, no accounts, no analytics, and not one third-party SDK in the binary. Your collection lives on your device and syncs only through your own private iCloud. Read the exact claim, and its three exceptions.

The rotation graph

Not "you haven't worn this in a while."

Every morning ROTA runs a decision graph over your collection: occasion type × live weather × wear equity × strap configuration × your learned taste × what you actually chose last time for this kind of day. It proposes one hero pick on its plinth, with ranked alternates beneath, and a quiet "Why this pick?" that shows you the working.

Override it and it learns. Choose the Speedmaster over its suggestion three times for an office day and it acknowledges the pattern once, adjusts, and stops arguing.

With three watches or fewer the same engine scores watch and strap together, because a beginner's collection is their straps: one Aqua Terra on a bracelet, a grey NATO and a leather strap is already a six-mood wardrobe.

What is inside

A collection manager that behaves like a gallery.

The Gallery

A photography-first grid where every watch sits on a soft gradient plinth. No photograph yet? A typographic Specimen Card stands in — brand, collection and reference set like a museum caption — so an empty collection never looks broken.

The Box

A virtual watch box with room for one more: walnut at every tier, noir and saddle for Pro. Drag your watches into the slots they occupy in your head, not the order you happened to add them.

Straps as first-class objects

One strap dresses many watches, and it changes what a watch is for. The Santos on burgundy leather is a different animal from the Santos on bracelet, and ROTA scores it that way.

Wrist Time

Wear bars, a calendar heat-map, occasion history and a collection-level view: occasion coverage, variety, redundancy. Chrono24 tells you what your watches are worth. ROTA tells you what your collection is missing.

The Vitrine and the Journey

A drag-ranked dream list with a Next Purchase Fit score, framed as chapters rather than gaps — The One-Watch Life, The Classic Trio, The Five-Slot Box, The Curated Cabinet. Own nothing yet? The taste primer will shortlist your first watch.

Care and Chronicle

Service intervals, warranty expiry, water-resistance retests, and an accuracy logger that charts seconds per day. Chronicle keeps the wrist shots, and each January turns them into your year on the wrist.

The Journal

Headlines from the publishers you choose, matched to your watches on the device. Headline, source, date and a link to the publisher's own page — never their article text, never their images. Free at every tier, always.

PDF lookbook

A museum-caption catalogue of your collection, rendered on the device, for a peer, an insurer or an estate. Prices are off by default. Serial numbers are never included in a shared document at all.

Admire

Full-bleed, chrome-free, a slow light sweep across the dial and the time set small beneath it. Swipe through the collection like a private exhibition. On iPad it doubles as a desk stand.

Privacy

Your collection. Nobody's business.

ROTA has no servers, no accounts, and no analytics — we never receive your data.

That is the whole claim, and it is worded carefully. It does not say nothing leaves your phone; it says nothing reaches us. Three things do leave, and you should know all three by name: approximate location goes to Apple for the weather, your collection goes to your own private iCloud, and a Journal fetch tells the publishers you enable that a feed was read.

The App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected. There are no third-party SDKs in the binary at all — not for analytics, not for the paywall — which is what makes the sentence above literally true rather than merely intended.

Requirements

What you need.

  • iOS 26 or later, on iPhone or iPad. One purchase covers both.
  • An iCloud account if you want your collection on more than one device. Signed out, ROTA works perfectly well on a single device.
  • Nothing else. No account to create, no email to hand over, no network connection required for the daily suggestion.
