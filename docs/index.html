ROTA — watch collection

The most beautiful way
to wear your collection.

The daily ritual app for watch collectors. Every morning it answers one question with taste and context — which watch today? — wrapped in a gallery-grade collection manager, a taste engine and a shareable lookbook.

iPhone and iPad · iOS 26 · Coming to the App Store

Three promises

It knows your life, it knows your taste,
and it knows nothing about you.

It knows your life

Occasion, live weather, wear history and your learned preferences feed one suggestion. Rain rests your leather straps. Twenty-eight degrees favours a bracelet.

It knows your taste

Six scoring axes, weighted by you — old money, full bracelets, variety; not a fan of this brand — shape every suggestion and every "what next" answer.

It knows nothing about you

No servers, no accounts, no analytics, no third-party SDKs. Your collection lives on your device and syncs only through your own iCloud. The exact claim, and its three exceptions.

The rotation graph

Not "you haven't worn this in a while."

A decision graph runs over your collection each morning: occasion × weather × wear equity × straps × taste × what you chose last time. One hero pick on its plinth, ranked alternates beneath, and a quiet "Why this pick?" that shows the working.

Override it and it learns. Choose the Speedmaster over its suggestion three times for an office day and it acknowledges the pattern once, then stops arguing.

Under four watches it scores watch and strap together — an Aqua Terra with a bracelet, a grey NATO and a leather strap is already a six-mood wardrobe.

What is inside

A collection manager that behaves like a gallery.

The Gallery

Every watch on a soft gradient plinth. No photograph yet? A typographic Specimen Card stands in, set like a museum caption.

The Box

A virtual box with room for one more — walnut free, noir and saddle for Pro. Drag watches into the slots they occupy in your head.

Straps as first-class objects

A strap changes what a watch is for. The Santos on burgundy leather is a different animal from the Santos on bracelet.

Wrist Time

Wear bars, a calendar heat-map, occasion coverage and redundancy. Chrono24 tells you what your watches are worth; ROTA tells you what your collection is missing.

The Vitrine and the Journey

A drag-ranked dream list with a Next Purchase Fit score, framed as chapters rather than gaps — The One-Watch Life, The Classic Trio, The Five-Slot Box.

Care and Chronicle

Service intervals, warranty expiry, water-resistance retests — and a photo journal of what you actually wore.

The Journal

Headlines from publishers you choose, matched to your brands. Source and date only, linked out — the publisher gets the visit.

PDF lookbook

A museum-caption catalogue of your collection, typeset on-device. Values and serials stay off unless you ask.

Admire

Full-bleed, chrome-free, a slow light sweep across the dial. The screen you hand someone.

Privacy

Your collection. Nobody's business.

ROTA has no servers, no accounts, and no analytics — we never receive your data.

That is the whole claim, and it is worded carefully. It does not say nothing leaves your phone; it says nothing reaches us. Three things do leave, and you should know all three by name: approximate location goes to Apple for the weather, your collection goes to your own private iCloud, and a Journal fetch tells the publishers you enable that a feed was read.

The App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected. There are no third-party SDKs in the binary at all — not for analytics, not for the paywall — which is what makes the sentence above literally true rather than merely intended.

Requirements

What you need.

  • iOS 26 or later, on iPhone or iPad. One purchase covers both.
  • An iCloud account if you want your collection on more than one device. Signed out, ROTA works perfectly well on a single device.
  • Nothing else. No account to create, no email to hand over, no network connection required for the daily suggestion.
